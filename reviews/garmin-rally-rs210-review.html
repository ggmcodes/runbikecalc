Common questions

Pedal, crank or spider power meter?

Pedals move between bikes in minutes and are exposed in crashes. Crank and spider meters are protected and are a job to move. If you own one bike and will keep it, a crank meter is a fine choice; if you own several or expect to change, pedals are the answer.

Is dual sided power worth it?

It is if you have a genuine left right imbalance, because single sided meters double the left leg and assume symmetry. Most riders are within a few percent, in which case a single sided meter tracks training just as well. Dual sided removes the assumption, which matters after an injury.

Garmin Rally or Favero Assioma?

Comparable accuracy, and Favero is around four hundred dollars cheaper. Garmin adds cycling dynamics and lands the data natively in Connect. If you are deep in the Garmin ecosystem the integration is worth something; if not, the Favero saves real money. Our best power meter pedals roundup goes through both.

Will my power meter match my trainer?

Rarely exactly, and that is normal. Different devices measure at different points in the drivetrain. Pick one source as your reference for training and stick to it. Our FTP calculator works from whichever device you designate.

Do I need a specific head unit?

Any ANT plus or Bluetooth head unit will record power and cadence. Cycling dynamics specifically requires a compatible Garmin head unit to display, so if that data is why you are buying, check the pairing.
