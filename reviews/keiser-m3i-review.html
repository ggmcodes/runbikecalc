Keiser M3i Studio Plus review

Commercial grade, no subscription, no screen, and a power number you can trust.

The verdict: The M3i is a commercial spin bike that broadcasts standard power and cadence data to any app you like, with no subscription, no screen and no software that can be discontinued. Buy it if you want an indoor bike that will still work in ten years and you already use Zwift, TrainerRoad or a Garmin. Skip it if you want the classes, because there is no screen and the M3i's whole design assumes you bring your own software.

What it does well

  • Bluetooth FTMS and ANT plus mean the bike speaks the standard protocols, so Zwift, TrainerRoad, Wahoo SYSTM and a Garmin head unit all see it without an adapter or an account.
  • No subscription and no screen means nothing to be discontinued. Connected bikes with proprietary tablets become expensive furniture when the company changes its mind.
  • The magnetic resistance mechanism has no contact wear surface, which is why these bikes survive years of commercial gym use.
  • The rear mounted flywheel keeps sweat off the mechanism, which is a specific and unglamorous reason these last.

Where it falls short

  • It is a spin bike, not a smart trainer, so the resistance does not change automatically when a Zwift workout calls for it. You turn the lever yourself.
  • Power is calculated rather than measured by a strain gauge. It is consistent, which is what matters for training, and it will not read identically to a power meter on your road bike.
  • Three and a half thousand dollars with no screen is a hard sell next to a Peloton at less money, and the answer is longevity rather than features.
  • The riding position is a spin bike position, not a road bike fit, so it does not substitute for training on your own bike geometry.

What owners report

Published owner reports are dominated by longevity and by gyms: people who used M3is in studios and then bought one describe them as still feeling new after years. The absence of a subscription comes up repeatedly as the reason for choosing it over a connected bike.

The criticism that recurs is power accuracy relative to a crank or pedal based meter, which is expected for a calculated figure. Experienced owners describe treating the M3i's numbers as internally consistent and not comparing them to outdoor watts, which is the correct approach and worth knowing before you buy.

Who it suits

  • Riders who already pay for Zwift, TrainerRoad or similar and want a bike that just works with it.
  • Anyone who wants equipment that outlives the company that sold it.
  • Households where several people ride and a commercial mechanism earns its price.

Who should skip it

  • People who want the class experience with a screen, where Peloton is the product.
  • Cyclists who want automatic resistance control for structured workouts, where a smart trainer with your own bike is better.
  • Anyone whose main goal is training for outdoor riding on their own bike fit.

Common questions

Keiser M3i or Peloton?

Different products. Peloton sells classes on a screen with a monthly membership; Keiser sells a commercial bike that broadcasts standard data and expects you to bring your own app. If motivation comes from instructors, Peloton. If it comes from a training plan, Keiser. Our Peloton versus Echelon versus Schwinn comparison covers the class bike market.

Is the power reading accurate?

It is consistent, which is what matters for training, and it is calculated from resistance and cadence rather than measured by a strain gauge. Do not expect it to match a pedal power meter on your road bike. Use our FTP calculator with the M3i's own numbers rather than mixing sources.

Does it work with Zwift?

Yes, over Bluetooth FTMS or ANT plus. Zwift will see power and cadence, though it cannot control resistance, so you ride in a non ERG mode and change the lever yourself. That is a real difference from a smart trainer and it is the main functional limitation.

Spin bike or smart trainer?

A smart trainer puts your own bike under you with your own saddle and fit, and controls resistance automatically. A spin bike is a self contained machine that needs no setup and suits a household where several people ride. Our smart trainer versus spin bike comparison works through it.

Do I need a subscription?

No, and that is the point. The M3i has no proprietary software layer at all. You can ride it with nothing but the console, or pair it with any app you already pay for.
