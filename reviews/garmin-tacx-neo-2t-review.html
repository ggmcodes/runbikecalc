Garmin Tacx Neo 2T review

Thirty two neodymium magnets, no calibration, no wear surface, and no motion plates.

The verdict: The Neo 2T applies resistance with thirty two magnets and no contact surface, which means no calibration, no drift and nothing to wear out. Buy it if you want the most reliable power numbers indoors without paying for motion plates. What it does well

  • No calibration is the underrated feature. A trainer that drifts as it warms up produces workouts you cannot compare week to week, and this one simply does not.
  • No contact between the resistance unit and the drivetrain means no belt to slip and no roller to wear, which is why these last.
  • It folds, which matters in a room that has to be a room again after the ride.
  • ANT plus and Bluetooth together mean it pairs with a Garmin head unit and a tablet at the same time, which is how most people actually ride indoors.

Where it falls short

  • No motion plates. The bike is held rigid, and on rides beyond an hour that is felt in the hands, the saddle and the lower back.
  • Eleven hundred dollars is still a lot next to trainers that train you identically for half the price.
  • Direct drive means removing the rear wheel and buying a cassette that matches your drivetrain, which is a setup cost and a job.
  • It is heavy. Folding helps with storage and not with carrying it up stairs.

What owners report

Published owner reports describe the Neo family as the most set and forget trainers available, with power consistency the recurring reason. Silence is mentioned frequently, since without a belt or roller the main noise is the drivetrain itself.

The criticism is price and the absence of movement. Reports from riders who upgraded to a NEO with motion plates describe the difference as comfort rather than data, which is consistent with what Garmin claims and is the right way to think about the gap.

Who it suits

  • Riders who want power numbers they never have to think about.
  • Anyone in a flat or shared house where trainer noise matters.
  • Cyclists who want a trainer that will still be working in five years.

Who should skip it

  • Riders doing long sessions who would benefit from lateral movement.
  • Anyone on a budget, where the KICKR CORE 2 trains identically for half.
  • Riders who would rather buy a power meter that works outdoors.

Common questions

Why does calibration matter?

Because trainers that measure power indirectly drift as they warm up, and a workout done on a cold trainer is not comparable to one done on a hot trainer. The Neo measures electromagnetically, so there is no spin down and no drift. That consistency is what makes week to week training data meaningful.

Is the Neo 2T quiet?

Very, because there is no belt and no roller touching the drivetrain. The noise you hear is your own chain and cassette. In a flat or a shared house that is a genuine consideration and it is the main reason people choose direct drive over a wheel on trainer.

Do I need a cassette?

Yes, for the Neo, and it must match your drivetrain speed and brand. Fitting one needs a chain whip and a lockring tool or a bike shop. Wahoo's KICKR CORE 2 ships with the Zwift Cog specifically to avoid this, which is worth knowing if you would rather not.

Will my trainer FTP match my outdoor FTP?

Usually not exactly, and it is normal for indoor power to read a little lower. Keep separate figures rather than reconciling them. Our FTP calculator works from a test on whichever setup you are using, and our training stress calculator uses that figure for load.

Neo 2T or a power meter?

A power meter measures your output wherever you ride and is the better first purchase if you race or train outdoors. A smart trainer controls resistance for indoor structure. Our power meter versus smart trainer comparison covers the order to buy them in.
