What it does well

  • Thirty one hours of GPS is more than the 970's twenty six, which is an unusual case of the cheaper watch winning on the specification that matters most for long events.
  • Full colour built in maps at this price means you can navigate an unfamiliar route without a phone, which for trail running is a safety feature rather than a convenience.
  • Multi band GPS materially improves accuracy in cities and under tree cover, which is where single band watches produce the pace spikes runners complain about.
  • Training readiness is the same score the flagship produces, and it is the metric most likely to change your training.

Where it falls short

  • It does not have the newest running economy and step speed loss metrics, which is the whole difference against the 970.
  • Forty seven millimetres is a large watch, and there is no smaller case in this model.
  • An AMOLED display in always on mode consumes battery far faster than the headline numbers suggest, so those figures assume gesture activation.
  • As with every wrist watch, heart rate during intervals is the weak point, and a chest strap improves everything derived from it.

What owners report

Published owner reports describe the 965 as the watch that finally combined an AMOLED display with maps and a battery that lasts, and it is widely recommended as the value pick in Garmin's running range. Map navigation draws consistent praise from trail runners.

The criticism is size and the always on display battery cost, both of which are inherent to the design. Reports comparing it with the 970 generally conclude that the newer metrics are interesting rather than necessary, which is why the 965 remains recommended.

Who it suits

  • Serious runners and triathletes wanting maps, accuracy and training analysis.
  • Trail runners who need on wrist navigation.
  • Anyone who wants flagship features without flagship pricing.

Who should skip it

  • Runners who specifically want running economy and step speed loss.
  • Ultrarunners needing more than 31 hours of GPS.
  • Small wrists, since 47mm is the only size.

Common questions

Forerunner 965 or 970?

The 965 costs two hundred and forty dollars less and has longer GPS battery. The 970 adds running economy, step speed loss, a brighter display and a flashlight. Unless those metrics are the reason you are shopping, the 965 is the better buy and is widely recommended as such.

Do I need maps on a running watch?

For road running, no. For trail running and for exploring unfamiliar routes, they are genuinely valuable and occasionally a safety feature. Multi band GPS matters more universally, because it removes the pace spikes that single band watches produce in cities and under trees.

How accurate is the battery claim?

Twenty three days assumes gesture activated display and modest use of sensors. Running an always on AMOLED display with pulse oximetry and music reduces it substantially. Thirty one hours of GPS is the number that matters for events and is broadly reliable.

Should I add a chest strap?

For interval and threshold sessions, yes. Wrist optical heart rate lags on rapid changes and every training metric is built on that signal, so improving the input improves the training readiness score, the load figures and the zones. Our heart rate zone calculator uses whichever source you trust.

Is Garmin better than Coros or Polar?

Garmin has the deepest ecosystem and the most metrics. Coros generally offers longer battery for less money. Polar has a distinctive and well regarded training load model. Our Garmin versus Coros comparison covers the differences that matter in daily use.
