Common questions

Does solar charging on a bike computer actually work?

It contributes rather than replaces. Garmin quotes up to 100 hours in battery saver mode with solar and 45 hours in demanding use, and the real gain depends on how much sun the unit sees. For a multi day ride it is meaningful; for a two hour training ride it is irrelevant.

What is multi band GNSS and why does it matter?

It receives satellite signals on more than one frequency, which lets the unit reject reflections off buildings and cliffs. That is why multi band units produce sensible distance and pace in cities and valleys where single band units produce spikes. It is the most useful accuracy improvement in years.

Edge 1040 or Edge 850?

Battery and screen size. The 850 has preloaded maps, smart fuelling alerts and up to 36 hours in battery saver mode for a hundred and seventy dollars less. Unless you ride events longer than a day, the 850 does everything the 1040 does.

Garmin, Wahoo or Hammerhead?

Garmin has the deepest feature set and the best routing; Wahoo has the simplest setup through a phone; Hammerhead has the best screen and map rendering. Our Garmin versus Wahoo versus Hammerhead comparison sets out the differences that show up in daily use.

Do I need a bike computer if I have a watch?

Not strictly, and a bar mounted screen is far easier to read while riding, and gives navigation you can follow at speed. If you ride long distances or navigate, a head unit earns its place. If you ride known routes, a watch is enough.
