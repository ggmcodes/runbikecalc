What it does well

  • A hooked rim rather than hookless means the wheelset takes a far wider range of tyres, including high pressure clinchers, which hookless rims restrict for safety reasons.
  • Fifty millimetres is the depth where aerodynamic gain and crosswind handling balance for most riders on most roads.
  • Shimano's global service network means a damaged hub or a broken spoke is a repair rather than an ordeal, which is not true of every carbon wheel brand.
  • Ultegra sits close enough to Dura-Ace in performance that the price gap is mostly about weight, which for most riders is the least important variable.

Where it falls short

  • Fifteen hundred and seventy dollars is a lot of money for aerodynamic gains measured in a few watts at typical road speeds, and the honest gain scales with how fast you go.
  • Fifty millimetres in a strong crosswind is noticeable, particularly for lighter riders on exposed roads, and no marketing changes that.
  • Carbon rims and disc brakes together mean braking is not a rim issue any more, and a carbon rim is still more expensive to replace after an impact.
  • Tubeless setup is fiddly the first time, and a wheelset like this deserves proper tubeless tyres rather than tubes, which adds cost.

What owners report

Published owner reports describe a fast, stiff and well made wheelset, and Shimano's build quality and hub durability draw consistent praise. Riders coming from aluminium wheels describe a clear difference in how the bike holds speed.

The recurring caution is crosswind behaviour, which is a property of rim depth rather than a fault. Reports from lighter riders and from those on exposed coastal or mountain roads more often suggest a shallower rim, which is sensible advice.

Who it suits

Common questions

Is 50mm the right rim depth?

For most riders on most roads, yes. Shallower rims handle crosswinds better and are slightly lighter; deeper rims are faster in a straight line at higher speeds. Fifty millimetres is where the two curves cross for typical road speeds, which is why it is the most common depth sold.

Hooked or hookless rims?

Hooked rims take a far wider range of tyres and pressures. Hookless rims are lighter and impose tyre width and pressure limits for safety. This wheelset is hooked, which means fewer restrictions on what you can fit and is a genuine practical advantage.

How much faster are carbon wheels?

At typical road speeds the aerodynamic saving over a shallow aluminium wheel is real and modest, and it grows with speed. Tyre choice and pressure often produce a comparable saving for a fraction of the money. Our drafting power calculator shows how much larger the aerodynamic effects around you are.

Are they safe in crosswinds?

Fifty millimetres is manageable for most riders and is noticeable in gusts, particularly for lighter riders on exposed roads. If your regular routes are windy and open, a shallower rim is the more comfortable choice and you will lose very little.

Should I buy wheels or a power meter?

A power meter, almost always, if you do not have one. Wheels make you slightly faster for the same effort; a power meter makes your training more effective, and that produces a bigger change over a season. Our power to weight ratio calculator shows what actually moves the needle on a climb.
