Peloton Treadmill review

A 24 inch swivel screen, a 300 lb capacity, and a $49.99 monthly fee that never stops.

The verdict: The Peloton Tread is a well built treadmill wrapped around a class library, and the honest way to price it is the machine plus $49.99 a month for as long as you own it. Buy it if instructor led classes are what actually gets you running and you will use them. Skip it if you are a runner following a training plan, because a NordicTrack or Horizon gives you the same running for less and your plan does not need a personality.

What it does well

  • The swivel screen is genuinely useful. Stepping off the belt to do strength or yoga against the same subscription is what makes the membership feel worth paying rather than an extra bill.
  • Class based motivation works for a large number of people, and a treadmill that gets used beats a better treadmill that does not.
  • Build quality and the running surface are widely reported as excellent, and it is a proper treadmill rather than a screen bolted to a cheap deck.
  • Personalised weekly plans remove the decision fatigue that stops people training, which is a real and underrated benefit.

Where it falls short

  • The membership is the real cost. At $49.99 a month, five years of ownership adds roughly three thousand dollars, which is another treadmill.
  • Without the membership the hardware is substantially less useful, so you are buying a subscription with a treadmill attached rather than the other way round.
  • A 6 foot 4 and 300 lb limit is not unusual and it does exclude some runners, so check the specification against your household.
  • Runners following a structured plan get nothing from a class library, and are paying for content they will not open.

What owners report

Published owner reports about the hardware are consistently positive: the belt, the deck and the screen all draw praise, and reports of it feeling like a commercial machine are common. Motivation is the recurring theme in favourable reports.

The criticism concentrates entirely on the membership: its cost, its necessity and the fact that it never stops. Reports from experienced runners frequently note that they stopped using the classes within months and continued paying, which is the pattern to be honest with yourself about before buying.

Who it suits

  • People who need instructor led motivation to train consistently.
  • Households where several people will use one membership.
  • Anyone who will genuinely use the strength and yoga content off the belt.

Who should skip it

  • Runners following a structured plan, who are paying for content they will not open.
  • Anyone unwilling to commit to $49.99 a month indefinitely.
  • Runners over 6 foot 4 or 300 lb, who are outside the stated limits.

Common questions

Do you need the Peloton membership?

For anything beyond a basic manual run, yes: Peloton is explicit that the All-Access Membership unlocks the content and applicable features. Budget $49.99 a month for as long as you own the machine. Over five years that is roughly three thousand dollars, which is the honest total to compare against other treadmills.

Is the Peloton Tread good for marathon training?

The hardware is capable, and the class format is not built around structured endurance plans. If you are following a plan with prescribed paces and long runs, you are paying for entertainment you will mostly ignore. Our treadmill pace calculator converts your outdoor paces to belt speeds whichever machine you buy.

Peloton Tread or NordicTrack?

NordicTrack undercuts it on hardware price and on subscription cost while offering a similar screen and a wider incline range. Peloton has the stronger class library and instructors. If the classes are the reason you are buying, Peloton; otherwise NordicTrack is the better value.

Is treadmill running as good as outdoor running?

For aerobic training, close, with some differences in mechanics and effort at the same pace. The usual convention is a small incline to approximate outdoor effort. Our treadmill versus outdoor running guide covers what the research actually supports.

How much space does it need?

Sixty eight inches long by thirty three wide by sixty five high, plus clearance behind for safety. Treadmills need more room than the footprint suggests, and the space behind the belt is not optional.
