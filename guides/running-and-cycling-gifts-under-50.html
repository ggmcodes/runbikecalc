Twenty-six gifts, none over fifty dollars, all of them used

Everything on this page is under fifty dollars, and everything on it is the version of the thing that runners and cyclists recommend to each other. That distinction matters at this price. A cheap running sock blisters and a cheap bike light dies in a month; a Balega Hidden Comfort socks (3-pack) three-pack and a Cateye ViZ450 rear light are what people replace them with. If you want a bigger gift to build around, our gifts for runners and gifts for cyclists guides start at the top of the price range and work down to this one.

A good under-$50 gift for an endurance athlete does one of three things: it gets used every session (socks, bottles, a belt), it solves a safety problem they are ignoring (a headlamp, a rear light), or it is a consumable they buy constantly anyway (gels, balm, patches). How to turn a $50 gift into a great one

  • Bundle by problem. A SPIbelt Original running belt, GU Energy Gel mixed box (24) and Body Glide Original anti-chafe balm together is a complete long-run kit. Crankbrothers M19 multi-tool, Park Tool TL-1.2 tire levers, Park Tool GP-2 patch kit and Lezyne Pocket Drive HP mini pump together is a complete roadside kit. Either bundle reads as thoughtful rather than random.
  • Add the numbers. A chest strap is a better gift with a note pointing to our heart-rate zone calculator; a race belt is better with a link to the race pace calculator and their goal time.
  • Buy the consumables in the size they use. Gel boxes and balm get finished; socks and bottles get worn out. All of it is fine to give twice.
