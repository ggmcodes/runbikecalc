Gift ideas for triathletes: three sports, one wrist

Triathletes are the easiest endurance athletes to buy for and the easiest to get wrong. Easy, because three sports means three lists of things they need. Wrong, because the tri-specific items (wetsuits, tri suits, aero helmets, bike shoes) are all sized and personal. So this guide leans on the gifts that are one-size and high impact: the multisport watch, the trainer, the power meter, swim tech and recovery. Most expensive first, because a Garmin Forerunner 965 changes a season and a race belt changes a transition.

To size the gift: a triathlete doing sprint and Olympic races on a basic watch gets the most from a multisport watch. A half or full-distance athlete who already has a Garmin gets the most from the bike side (trainer, power, aero hydration) and recovery. If you know their FTP or run pace, our gift finder will pick the tier.

The big gift: the multisport watch, the trainer, the power meter

The watch first. A triathlon watch needs open-water swim tracking, one-button transitions and battery for the whole race; the Garmin Forerunner 965 has all three and full maps, and it is the watch we recommend to most age-groupers. The Garmin Forerunner 970 is the flagship. The Garmin Fenix 8 Sapphire is for the triathlete who wants one device for triathlon, hiking and travel. All three run our triathlon training plans straight from the calculator's zone output.

Then the bike. The bike leg is the longest and the one where pacing decides the run. A Wahoo KICKR CORE 2 (Zwift Cog and Click) trainer makes winter intervals possible; an Favero Assioma Duo pedal power meter makes race-day pacing possible; an Garmin Edge 850 fits between the aerobars. Check before buying: the Assioma uses Look Keo cleats, and a tri bike with a road bike in the garage means the pedal-based meter is the right one because it moves between them.

What to check before you buy

  • Skip the sized items unless you know the size. Wetsuits, tri suits, bike shoes and aero helmets are personal. Watches, trainers, power meters, goggles, bags and swim tools are not.
  • Bike compatibility. The Wahoo KICKR CORE 2 (Zwift Cog and Click) fits any bike with the included cog. The Favero Assioma Duo needs Look Keo cleats. The Profile Design FC35 aero bottle mounts between aerobars.
  • Race calendar. A triathlete with a full-distance race in spring is in a base block over the holidays; that is when a trainer and recovery gear pay off most.
  • Set up their zones. Send them to our heart-rate zone and LTHR zone calculators so the new watch is training them properly from the first session.

Questions people ask before buying

What is the best gift for a triathlete?

A multisport GPS watch, if they do not have a good one: the Garmin Forerunner 965 covers open-water swim, one-button transitions, full maps and 31 hours of GPS battery. If they already have a watch, a Wahoo KICKR CORE 2 (Zwift Cog and Click) smart trainer or Favero Assioma Duo power meter for the bike leg is the next most useful gift.

What should I get a triathlete who has everything?

Swim technology: FORM Smart Swim 2 goggles goggles, Shokz OpenSwim Pro headphones or a FINIS Tempo Trainer Pro. Recovery: Hyperice Normatec Elite boots or a Theragun Prime (6th Gen). Transition-day gear like a Zoot Ultra Tri Bag is the other gap in most kits.

Is the Forerunner 965 or the 970 better for triathlon?

Both have full multisport support. The Garmin Forerunner 970 adds a brighter screen, sapphire glass, a flashlight and ECG; the Garmin Forerunner 965 has longer GPS battery (31 vs 26 hours) and costs a lot less. For most age-group triathletes the 965 is the better value; the 970 is the gift if you want the flagship.

Are triathlon gifts under $50 worth it?

Yes: a race belt, elastic laces, a swim buoy, racing goggles and anti-chafe balm are the things triathletes forget until race week. The Speedo Teamster 35L backpack backpack at just over fifty is the practical transition bag.

Can I buy a wetsuit or tri suit as a gift?

Only if you know their exact size and brand. Wetsuit fit is unforgiving and returns are awkward. A watch, trainer, power meter, goggles or a transition bag are one-size and a much safer gift.
