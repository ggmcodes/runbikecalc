U-Lock vs Folding Lock

Locks are rated by how long they resist attack, and a lock that is easy to carry is a lock you will actually bring.

Verdict

A hardened U-lock gives the most resistance per pound and per pound sterling, which is why it remains the standard recommendation. A folding lock gives up some of that in exchange for reaching around objects a U-lock will not fit and packing flat on the frame. The best lock is the one you carry, and neither of them helps if the bike is locked badly.

No lock is unbreakable. Security ratings such as Sold Secure and ART describe how long a lock resists a determined attack with tools, and the practical aim is to make your bike a worse target than the one next to it and to take longer than a thief is willing to spend in public.

The other half is technique, and it matters as much as the hardware. A very good lock through only the front wheel protects a front wheel.

How to lock a bike properly

Lock the frame to something immovable, not just a wheel, and check that whatever you are locking to cannot be lifted over or unbolted. Fill the inside of the lock so there is no room for leverage. Keep the lock off the ground, since a lock on the pavement can be smashed against it. Secure both wheels with a second lock or a cable, remove lights and computers, and photograph the bike and record its serial number, since police recover bikes they cannot return without one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most secure bike lock?

A hardened steel U-lock with a high independent security rating, such as Sold Secure Gold or ART, is generally the best resistance for the weight and price. No lock is unbreakable; ratings describe how long the lock resists a determined attack with tools.

Are folding locks secure?

Good ones are rated well and are considerably better than a cable lock. They have more joints and rivets than a U-lock, which is where an attack is directed. Their advantage is reach and packability, which matters if it means you carry a proper lock instead of a light one.

Should I use two locks?

In a city, yes. Two locks of different types force a thief to carry and use two sets of tools, which is a meaningful deterrent, and a second lock covers the wheels that the first one does not. A common arrangement is a U-lock through the frame and rear wheel plus a second lock or cable for the front.

Does bike insurance cover theft?

Often, and usually with conditions about the lock's security rating and how the bike was secured. Policies frequently require a specific rating and proof of purchase for both bike and lock. Read the conditions before relying on it, and keep receipts and the serial number.
