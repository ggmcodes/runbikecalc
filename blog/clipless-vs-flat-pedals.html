Clipless vs Flat Pedals

Clipless pedals do not let you pull up meaningfully, and that is not why they are better.

Verdict

Clipless pedals are worth it for road riding, and not for the reason usually given. Studies of pedalling technique consistently find that riders do not produce meaningful power on the upstroke even when clipped in. The real benefits are that your foot stays exactly where you put it and that you can use a stiff-soled shoe. Flats are better for short trips, technical riding where you may need to dab, and anyone still nervous about being attached to the bike.

The pull-up myth is worth clearing first. When researchers have measured pedal forces, the upstroke leg is generally still pushing down slightly rather than pulling up, even in trained cyclists, and deliberately pulling up tends to reduce gross efficiency rather than improve it. Being clipped in does not add a power phase.

What it does add is foot location and stiffness. Your foot cannot move on the pedal, which means the fit you set stays set, and a stiff carbon or nylon sole transmits force without the sole flexing around the pedal. Both matter over hours.

The falling over phase is real

Nearly everyone who switches to clipless has at least one slow-speed topple, usually at a stop, and the way to keep it harmless is to expect it. Set release tension to minimum, practise on grass or against a wall, and consciously unclip early as you approach junctions until it is automatic. Cleat position matters for knees: too far forward or badly rotated cleats cause knee pain, and persistent knee pain after switching is a reason to see a bike fitter rather than to push through.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do clipless pedals make you faster?

Not through pulling up, which measurement studies consistently show riders do not do meaningfully. They help by keeping the foot located and by allowing a stiff sole, which matters over long rides and on efforts out of the saddle. The gain is real and smaller than the marketing suggests.

Are flat pedals bad for cycling?

No. They are the wrong tool for long road rides where foot position and sole stiffness matter, and they are a perfectly good choice for commuting, short trips and technical mountain biking. Many accomplished riders use flats deliberately.

Why are they called clipless when you clip in?

Because they replaced toe clips, the cages and straps that came before them. The name refers to the absence of the cage rather than to the absence of attachment, which is confusing and permanent.

How do I stop falling over when learning?

Set the release tension to its loosest setting, practise clipping in and out while leaning against a wall, then on grass, and unclip early as you approach any stop until it becomes automatic. Almost everyone topples at least once; low tension and early unclipping keep it undramatic.
