Floor Pump vs CO2 Inflator

One lives at home and does the real job; the other lives in your saddlebag for the twenty minutes that matter.

Verdict

These are not alternatives. Own a floor pump for home, because accurate pressure before every ride is the single cheapest thing you can do for comfort, grip and puncture resistance. Carry a CO2 inflator for punctures, because it fills a tyre in seconds at the roadside. Note that CO2 diffuses through tyre casings faster than air, so a CO2-filled tyre will be soft by the next day.

Tyre pressure is one of the few free performance and comfort variables on a bike, and most people ride under-inflated because they check by squeezing. A gauge on a floor pump removes the guesswork, and correct pressure meaningfully reduces pinch flats and improves both grip and rolling resistance.

A CO2 inflator solves a different problem entirely: getting a repaired tyre up to a rideable pressure quickly when you are standing at the side of a road. It is not a substitute for a pump at home and it is not economical to use routinely.

What to actually carry

A spare tube, two tyre levers, an inflator with two cartridges or a mini pump, and a means of calling for help. A mini pump is slower and heavier than CO2 and never runs out, which is why plenty of riders carry both. Re-inflate a CO2-filled tyre with a floor pump when you get home, since CO2 permeates the casing and the tyre will be noticeably soft within a day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does CO2 leak out of tyres so fast?

Carbon dioxide molecules permeate the rubber and latex of tyre casings and inner tubes considerably faster than the nitrogen and oxygen in air. A tyre filled with CO2 will typically be noticeably soft within 12 to 24 hours, which is fine for finishing a ride and not fine for leaving overnight.

How often should I check tyre pressure?

Before most rides, or at least weekly. Road tyres lose pressure noticeably over a few days simply through permeation, and running under-inflated is the main cause of pinch flats as well as of a slow, harsh ride. It takes under a minute with a floor pump.

Can I use a CO2 inflator on tubeless tyres?

Yes, and there is a caveat: CO2 can cause some sealants to congeal, and many riders replace the sealant afterwards. Some inflators are sold specifically for tubeless use. Check what your sealant manufacturer says.

Do I need a mini pump as well?

Many riders carry both. A CO2 cartridge is single use, so a second puncture on the same ride leaves you stranded unless you carry more cartridges. A mini pump is slower and heavier and never runs out, which is why it is the more reliable backup.
