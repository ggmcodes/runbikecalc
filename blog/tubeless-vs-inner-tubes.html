Tubeless vs Inner Tubes

Tubeless removes most punctures and adds a maintenance job you did not previously have.

Verdict

Tubeless is worth it if you ride gravel, ride in thorny country, or want lower pressures for comfort and grip, because sealant closes small punctures while you keep riding. Inner tubes are worth it if you want a system with no maintenance schedule, no mess and a roadside repair that always works. Tubeless trades frequent small annoyances for occasional larger ones.

The case for tubeless is genuine. Sealant seals the thorn and glass punctures that make up most flats, without you noticing, and removing the tube removes pinch flats, which lets you run lower pressures for real gains in comfort and grip on rough surfaces.

The case against is that you have signed up for a maintenance schedule. Sealant dries out and needs topping up every few months, initial setup can be a fight, and when a tubeless tyre does fail badly it fails messily, at the roadside, covered in latex. Carry a tube regardless.

Carry a tube either way

Tubeless does not remove the need for a spare tube. Sealant will not close a sidewall tear or a cut larger than a few millimetres, and when that happens the fix is to remove the valve core and fit a tube, in sealant, at the roadside. Carry a tube, levers and an inflator whatever you run. Check sealant levels every few months, since dried-out sealant gives you all the complexity of tubeless with none of the benefit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is tubeless worth it for road riding?

It depends on where you ride. On rough roads, in thorny country, or if you want lower pressures for comfort, yes. On smooth roads at normal pressures the puncture rate difference is less dramatic, and the maintenance is a real cost. Gravel riding is where the case is strongest.

How often do I top up tubeless sealant?

Typically every two to six months depending on the sealant, the climate and how much the bike is ridden, with hot dry conditions drying it faster. A tyre with no wet sealant in it is a tubeless tyre with all of the complexity and none of the protection.

Can you fit a tube in a tubeless tyre?

Yes, and it is the standard roadside fix for a puncture too large to seal. Remove the valve core and the valve, pull out whatever plug you tried, and fit the tube. It is messy, since the tyre is full of sealant, so carrying a glove or a rag is worth it.

Does tubeless save weight?

Marginally, and it varies. Removing the tube saves weight, and sealant and tubeless-specific tyres and rim tape add some back. Weight is not the reason to go tubeless; puncture resistance and the ability to run lower pressures are.
