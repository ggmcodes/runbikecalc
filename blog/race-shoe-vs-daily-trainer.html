Race Shoe vs Daily Trainer

A plated speed shoe and a cushioned trainer are not interchangeable, and only one of them should carry your weekly mileage.

Verdict

Buy the daily trainer first and add a race shoe only when you are racing. Plated speed shoes are light, firm and fast, and they wear out in roughly half the miles of a normal trainer, which makes them an expensive way to run easy days. The trainer is the shoe that should carry most of your week.

Plated shoes deserve their reputation. Published testing on the carbon-plated super shoe category has found running economy improvements of around four percent for many runners, which is a large effect in a sport where most things move performance by fractions of a percent. That effect is real and it is also specific: it shows up at race paces, in shoes that are built light and firm to produce it.

The cost is durability. Plated racers are commonly cited at 150 to 250 miles of useful life against 300 to 500 for a normal trainer, and they cost more per pair. Running easy miles in them is the most expensive way to run easy miles.

Introduce a plated shoe gradually

Plated shoes change how load is distributed through the foot and lower leg, and a common pattern is calf and Achilles soreness in the first few runs. Do not race in a pair you have never run in. If you develop pain that persists beyond ordinary soreness, particularly localised pain in the foot or Achilles, see a physiotherapist or sports doctor rather than running through it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do carbon plate shoes make you faster?

Published testing on the super shoe category has found running economy improvements of around four percent for many runners at race paces, which translates to a meaningful time difference over a marathon. The effect varies between individuals and it is much smaller at easy paces, which is part of why they are race shoes rather than everyday shoes.

How long do racing shoes last?

Commonly cited figures are 150 to 250 miles for a plated racer against 300 to 500 for a standard trainer. The foam in fast shoes is lighter and less durable by design. Reserving them for workouts and races is what makes the cost tolerable.

Do I need two pairs of running shoes?

Not to start. If you are adding a second pair, a second daily trainer to alternate does more for shoe longevity and for your legs than a race shoe does, unless you are actually racing. Rotating two trainers lets the foam recover between runs.

Can I use a race shoe for every run?

You can, and it is expensive and probably unwise. The firm plated ride loads the calf and Achilles differently, and doing all your mileage that way is a common route to lower leg complaints. Save them for the sessions where the benefit exists.
