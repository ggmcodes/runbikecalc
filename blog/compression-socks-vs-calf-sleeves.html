Compression Socks vs Calf Sleeves

Sleeves let you choose your own running sock; full socks add graduated compression at the ankle and foot.

Verdict

Wear calf sleeves while running, because they let you keep the running sock you already like, and full compression socks afterwards or on a long flight, where graduated ankle-to-calf pressure is what the evidence actually supports. The research on compression consistently shows reduced perceived soreness and swelling, and very little effect on performance.

Compression is one of the better studied bits of running kit and the findings are consistent and modest. Graduated compression garments reduce swelling and the feeling of soreness after hard efforts, and reviews generally find no meaningful improvement in running performance while wearing them. Neither of these will make you faster.

The difference between the two formats is what they cover. A sleeve compresses the calf only, so your foot is in whatever sock you choose. A full sock adds graduated compression from the ankle upward, which is the arrangement used in the medical literature on venous return.

Sizing and when not to wear compression

Compression is sized by measurement, not by shoe size, and a garment that rolls, digs in or leaves deep marks is the wrong size rather than the right one worn badly. Do not use compression if you have a known or suspected blood clot, severe peripheral arterial disease, or an open wound on the limb, and speak to a clinician first if you have diabetes with neuropathy or any circulatory condition. Pressures above 20 to 30 mmHg are medical grade and should be prescribed rather than self selected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do compression socks improve running performance?

Reviews generally find no meaningful improvement in running performance from wearing compression. What they do find consistently is reduced perceived soreness and reduced swelling after hard efforts, which is a real benefit even though it is not a performance one.

Should I wear compression during or after running?

Both are common and the post-exercise evidence is the stronger of the two. Many runners wear sleeves during and full socks afterwards. If you are choosing one occasion, afterwards and on travel days is where the support is best.

What compression level do I need?

For athletic and travel use, 15 to 20 mmHg or 20 to 30 mmHg is the usual range. Higher pressures are medical grade and should be prescribed. Measure your calf rather than guessing, since fit determines whether the graduation works at all.

Do calf sleeves help shin splints?

Some runners find they reduce discomfort, and there is no good evidence that compression treats or prevents medial tibial stress syndrome. Shin pain that persists, worsens or becomes focal needs assessment, since a stress fracture presents similarly and running through one makes it considerably worse.
