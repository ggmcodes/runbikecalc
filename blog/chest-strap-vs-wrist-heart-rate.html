Chest Strap vs Wrist Heart Rate

Optical wrist sensors are good at steady efforts and unreliable at exactly the moments training decisions depend on.

Verdict

If your training is driven by heart rate zones, use a chest strap. Electrode-based straps measure the heart's electrical signal directly and track rapid changes accurately; optical wrist sensors infer rate from blood flow and are known to lag during intervals, drift in the cold and misread on rapid changes. For steady running and for general daily tracking, the wrist is fine.

The two technologies are not doing the same thing. A chest strap is a simplified electrocardiogram: it reads the electrical activity that triggers each beat. An optical wrist sensor shines light into the skin and infers a rate from changes in blood volume, which is a harder inference and one that is disturbed by movement, skin tone, temperature, sweat, tattoos and how tightly the watch is worn.

Validation studies consistently find good agreement at steady state and worse agreement during rapid changes in intensity, which is exactly where interval training lives.

What heart rate cannot tell you

Heart rate is affected by heat, dehydration, caffeine, illness, sleep and stress, so a session that feels harder than the numbers suggest usually reflects something real rather than a broken sensor. Cardiac drift means rate rises at a constant effort over a long run. If you experience chest pain, unexplained breathlessness, fainting, or a heart rate that is wildly out of line with your effort and stays there, stop and speak to a doctor rather than adjusting your zones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are wrist heart rate monitors accurate?

At steady effort, generally yes. During intervals, rapid accelerations and cold weather they are noticeably less reliable, with lag and occasional misreading of cadence as heart rate. Validation studies consistently show this pattern. For zone-based training, a chest strap removes the doubt.

Why does my watch show the wrong heart rate?

Common causes are a loose strap, wearing the watch too close to the wrist bone, cold hands reducing blood flow at the skin, sweat under the sensor and the sensor locking onto your cadence instead of your pulse. Tightening the band and moving it higher up the arm fixes most of it.

Do I need a chest strap for zone 2 training?

It helps, because zone 2 work depends on staying inside a fairly narrow band and an optical sensor that reads ten beats high will have you running the wrong session. If you are serious about aerobic base work, a strap is one of the cheapest meaningful upgrades you can make.

Can I use a chest strap with any watch?

Most modern straps broadcast over Bluetooth and ANT+, which covers essentially every current running watch and most bike computers, and Bluetooth also covers phone apps. Check that your watch supports external sensors, which nearly all running-focused watches do.
