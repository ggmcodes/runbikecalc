Wet vs Dry Chain Lube

Dry lube stays clean and washes off in rain; wet lube survives rain and collects everything on the road.

Verdict

Use dry lube in dry, dusty conditions and wet lube in rain and winter. Dry lube goes on wet, dries to a waxy film and keeps grit from sticking, but washes off in rain. Wet lube is an oil that stays put through water and picks up every bit of grit on the road, which is why it needs cleaning off rather than topping up.

Chain maintenance is not glamorous and it is the cheapest way to make a drivetrain last. A worn chain wears the cassette and chainrings, which turns a $30 replacement into a $200 one, and the main driver of chain wear is abrasive grit held against the rollers by lubricant.

That is the whole logic of the two categories. Dry lube minimises what sticks to the chain at the cost of durability in water. Wet lube maximises durability in water at the cost of collecting grit. Neither is better in general; they are matched to conditions.

Application is more important than the lube

Apply one drop per roller while backpedalling, let it sit for a few minutes to penetrate, then wipe the outside of the chain thoroughly. Lubricant belongs inside the rollers, not on the outside where it only collects grit. A visibly wet, black chain is over-lubricated and dirty rather than well maintained. Check chain wear with a chain checker: replacing a chain at 0.5 percent wear costs a fraction of replacing the cassette and chainrings that a stretched chain destroys.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I lube my bike chain?

With dry lube, roughly every 100 to 150 miles or after any dusty ride. With wet lube, less often, but clean and reapply after wet rides. The more useful signal is sound: a chain that has started to sound dry needs attention, and a chain that looks black and wet needs cleaning, not more lube.

Can I use WD-40 on my bike chain?

Standard WD-40 is a water-displacing solvent, not a chain lubricant, and it strips lubricant out of the rollers. It is fine for cleaning and useless as a lubricant. WD-40 does sell dedicated bike chain lubes, which are a different product entirely.

Do I need to degrease before relubing?

With dry lube, usually just a wipe with a rag between applications, with a proper degrease every few months. With wet lube, degreasing is more necessary because the accumulated grit needs to come off rather than be sealed in under fresh oil.

What about wax-based chain lube?

Immersion waxing keeps drivetrains extremely clean and shows very low wear rates in independent testing, at the cost of a fiddly process and short reapplication intervals. Drip-on wax lubes are a middle ground. It is a legitimate third option and considerably more effort than either of these.
